California thermal anomalies and aerosols

Aerosol optical depth (AOD) measures how much particles in the atmospheric column attenuate light. Higher values indicate more aerosol loading. It is not a ground-level air-quality measurement. Colors show approximate ranges decoded from MODIS imagery; gray areas have no usable observation.
MODIS detects satellite pixels with thermal anomalies, not separate fires. Orange dots have a constant screen size. Opacity increases with fire radiative power (FRP, in megawatts), reaching full opacity at 100 MW. Source types are inferred; recent records may have an unknown type.

Data and methods

AOD coverage begins February 24, 2000; thermal detections begin November 1, 2000. Dates without thermal observations are unavailable, not zero. The selectable range ends at the earlier of yesterday (UTC) and the downloaded snapshot. AOD is approximate; gray map areas have no usable observation. Detections use equal-size orange markers. Opacity is proportional to FRP, capped at full opacity at 100 MW; unknown power uses 50% opacity. Marker size stays constant on screen while zooming. Legend ranges show representative opacity; the mapping is continuous. Hotspot types are inferred, not confirmed causes. “Vegetation fire” means presumed vegetation fire. Points are detections, not distinct fires. NASA FIRMS annual MODIS records and recent GIBS Terra records are downloaded locally and loaded by day. AOD imagery is loaded live from GIBS. County borders and the base map are grayscale. Histogram counts are statewide and follow type and confidence filters, independently of the map selection.
